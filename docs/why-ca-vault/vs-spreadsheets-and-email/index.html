Why Construction Teams Are Moving Beyond Spreadsheets and Email

Spreadsheets and email are not bad tools. They are general tools doing a specialised job. Here is what changes, workflow by workflow, when the contract lives in one record instead.

Short answer: spreadsheets and email can hold construction administration data, but they cannot connect it. Each bid, RFI, change and invoice is a separate file that someone must reconcile by hand. A purpose-built platform stores them against the contract, so one entry updates everything that depends on it.

Where spreadsheets and email break down

The trouble is rarely the first tender or the first invoice. It is the fifth change order on the third contract, when the spreadsheet has been copied twice and nobody is sure which tab is current.

1. The same number is typed more than once

A unit price is entered by the bidder, re-entered by the consultant for the bid review, entered again in the contract spreadsheet, and again by the contractor in each invoice. Every re-entry is a chance for a mistake.

2. Approvals are separated from what they approve

An email saying “approved” sits in one person’s inbox. The change it approves is in a spreadsheet somewhere else. Linking the two later takes detective work.

3. Each party has its own version

The contractor’s quantities and the consultant’s quantities are kept separately and compared monthly. Differences are found at the worst time: when payment is due.

What changes with a connected record

  • Bids arrive comparable. Every bidder prices the same schedule, so the comparison exists the moment the tender closes.
  • Changes carry their paper trail. The quote, the signatures and the evidence stay with the change order.
  • Invoices and certificates agree. The certificate is produced from the invoice, which is produced from recorded quantities.
  • Nothing waits unseen. Items needing a response sit in an ActionBox with reminders, not an unread email.

When spreadsheets are still fine

If you administer one small lump-sum contract a year with a contractor you know well, a spreadsheet may be all you need. The case for a platform grows with the number of line items, bidders, changes and parties involved.
