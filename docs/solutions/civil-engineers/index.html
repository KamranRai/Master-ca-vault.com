S1.1 Where the time goes

Engineering hours spent on data entry

  • Bid review by retyping

    Hundreds of unit prices from several bidders, keyed into a spreadsheet before you can even start the analysis.

  • Monthly certificate rebuilds

    Re-entering the contractor’s invoice to check quantities you already have on record.

  • Answers buried in email

    RFI responses and change approvals that are hard to find when a claim arrives a year later.

S1.2 Your workflow in CA-Vault

A unit-price contract, start to finish

  1. Tender

    Issue the schedule of prices and tender documents to bidders.

  2. Compare and recommend

    Review the generated bid comparison and export it for the owner.

  3. Administer

    Respond to RFIs, review submittals and manage changes on the contract.

  4. Certify

    Review progress invoices and issue payment certificates.

What changes

  • Bid comparisons generated at tender close
  • Payment certificates produced from the contractor’s own invoice
  • One traceable record of RFIs, changes and approvals

S1.5 FAQ

Questions civil engineers ask

Is CA-Vault suited to unit-price civil contracts?

Yes. The platform is organised around the schedule of prices: items, quantities, units and unit rates. That structure runs from tender through to payment certification.

Can we export the bid comparison for our client?

Yes. The bid review can be exported to PDF or Excel.

Do contractors need to buy CA-Vault to bid?

No. Contractor invitations are unlimited and free for contractors on the Tender plan and above.
